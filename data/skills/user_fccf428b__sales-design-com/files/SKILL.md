# Design.com Platform Help

Design.com (**design.com**) is an **AI-first logo maker and brand-kit platform** — and the fact that reframes every
answer: it is **DesignCrowd's own AI subsidiary/successor**, a **sister to BrandCrowd**, and **not** the human
designer-contest marketplace at `/sales-designcrowd`. Same corporate DNA, opposite model: **AI + templates in
minutes**, not 25–100+ freelancers competing over days.

Workflow: **business name or short prompt** → AI infers the industry and returns concepts from a **very large template
library** → refine with keywords → customize in an editor **plus an AI chat editor that takes plain-language edits**
→ download **PNG/JPG + vector SVG/EPS/PDF** (GIF/MP4 when animated). A **brand kit** auto-applies the logo's colors
and type to **business cards, social posts/stories, email signatures, letterheads, flyers, merch, invoices, QR codes,
presentations**; the suite extends into an **AI website generator + domain**, **digital business card**,
**link-in-bio**, **AI background remover**, and **print fulfillment**.

**The two facts behind every misunderstanding.** First: there are **TWO separate logo libraries** — a curated
**"Free Logos" collection** (design.com/maker/free-logos) that is **genuinely 100% free**, **vector included, no
watermark**, under a worldwide irrevocable commercial license — and a **much larger premium library** needing a
**paid plan**. That's why Design.com's marketing ("free plan, no upgrade to download") and third-party reviews ("no
export on free") are *both* right: different libraries. Second: it's a **recurring SUBSCRIPTION**, not the
pay-once-per-logo model of the budget cluster — buyers who think they're "buying a logo" land on a renewing brand-kit
plan, the largest source of complaints. It is **UI-only — no public API.**

## Step 4 — Actionable guidance

- **Establish WHICH library the logo came from before answering anything about price.** Ask (or have the user check)
  whether the mark is from the curated **"Free Logos" collection** at design.com/maker/free-logos — free forever,
  **vector SVG/EPS/PDF + PNG/JPG, no watermark**, worldwide irrevocable commercial license — or the **much larger
  premium library**, needing a **paid plan**. Never answer "is Design.com free?" with a flat yes or no: it's
  **"free for one specific collection, paid for the rest."** If the user wants to spend nothing, **actively point them
  at design.com/maker/free-logos** as the zero-cost route rather than only diagnosing why they hit a paywall.
- **Say the word SUBSCRIPTION explicitly, before they pay.** Unlike most budget peers (one-time per logo), Design.com
  bills a **recurring plan** — monthly, or annually at a much lower effective rate that is **a year's commitment billed
  upfront** — and it **auto-renews**. That mismatch (expecting a one-time logo, getting a renewing brand-kit plan) is
  the **#1 complaint**; surface it unprompted.
- **Present pricing AND tier contents as best-effort, and send them to design.com to confirm — every time, including
  when the question is about formats or features rather than price.** Tier names, figures **and what each tier bundles
  disagree across sources** (Starter/Value/Premium in some reviews, a "Brand" plan in others). Quote a range, name each
  plan by what it *unlocks*, and close any tier/plan/format answer by telling the user to verify the current ladder,
  file set, and renewal terms at design.com.
- **Vector is not the gate here — the library is.** Every paid download *and* every **Free Logos** download includes
  **SVG, EPS, PDF** alongside PNG/JPG, so there's **no "upgrade for vector" trap** like Turbologo's. Use **vector for
  print/signage/apparel**, PNG/JPG for web/social; higher tiers add **scope**, not file quality. State the included
  file set as **best-effort and tell the user to confirm it at design.com** — the live pricing route wasn't reachable
  at research time, so the per-tier file list is third-party-sourced.
- **Separate the standard license from an exclusive/buyout license, and both from a trademark.** Every download grants
  a **worldwide irrevocable commercial license** and each logo passes a **manual IP review** — but a template mark is
  **non-exclusive by default**, and minimally-customized output looks templated (push real customization via the **AI
  chat editor**). Design.com sells **exclusive/buyout licenses on select logos that remove the icon from the library**
  — recommend that when uniqueness matters. A license is **not** a trademark: run **USPTO/EUIPO** + reverse-image first.
- **For billing problems, act on the download-state rule.** Cancellation is self-serve from the account dashboard,
  but **refund eligibility turns on whether the paid assets were already downloaded** and on the published window.
  Cancel immediately, keep receipts, request inside the window via design.com/maker/contact, then dispute with the card
  issuer if unresolved.
- **Don't confuse Design.com with DesignCrowd or BrandCrowd — name the difference out loud.** Same parent, different
  products: **Design.com** = AI/DIY subscription brand kit; **DesignCrowd** (`/sales-designcrowd`) = human
  designer-contest marketplace, pay upfront per project, winner transfers full copyright; **BrandCrowd** = sister
  template/AI maker. If a user says "DesignCrowd's AI tool," they mean Design.com.
- **There is NO public API — don't plan a pipeline around it.** Design.com is **UI-only**: no REST endpoints, no
  webhooks, no Zapier/Make, no MCP, no self-serve white-label/reseller program, so logo generation **cannot be
  scripted**. For bulk/programmatic work use an **image-generation service with a documented API** and vectorize
  downstream.

## Gotchas

*Best-effort from research (2026-07) — plan names, prices, renewal and refund terms move, and sources disagree on
the tier ladder. Treat every figure as best-effort and confirm at design.com.*

- **TWO libraries, one brand.** The curated **"Free Logos"** collection is genuinely free (vector + PNG/JPG, **no
  watermark**, commercial license); the **much larger premium library** requires a **paid plan**. Contradictory
  "it's free" / "you can't export on free" claims both trace to this split.
- **It's a SUBSCRIPTION, not a one-time logo purchase** — and it **auto-renews** (annual = a year committed upfront,
  far cheaper per month). Top complaint by far (PissedConsumer ~1.8★, Trustpilot + r/SmallBusinessOwners echo it).
  **Tier names and prices disagree across sources** — describe what a tier unlocks, never quote a ladder as fact.
- **Refunds hinge on whether you downloaded** — cancel self-serve from the dashboard; eligibility depends on download state and the published window. Screenshot everything, act fast.
- **Vector is included at every paid tier and on free logos** (SVG/EPS/PDF) — no vector upsell; higher tiers add **scope** (website + domain, digital business card, link-in-bio), not better files.
- **Default marks are non-exclusive despite manual IP review.** **Exclusive/buyout licenses** on select logos remove the icon from the library — the real uniqueness path, and barely-edited templates look templated (use the **AI chat editor** to differentiate). A license is still **not a trademark**: clear it (USPTO/EUIPO + reverse-image) first.
- **Not DesignCrowd, not BrandCrowd** — same parent, different models: AI subscription brand kit vs human contest marketplace vs sister template maker. **No public API, webhooks, Zapier/Make, MCP, or self-serve white-label** either — logo generation can't be scripted.

## Troubleshooting

### "Design.com won't let me download my logo without a plan"
**Symptom**: The logo is finished but the download demands a subscription.
**Cause**: The mark came from the **premium library**, not the curated **"Free Logos"** collection — Design.com runs
**two libraries**, and only the free collection downloads for free (vector + PNG/JPG, **no watermark**).
**Solution**: To spend nothing, pick a mark from **design.com/maker/free-logos** and edit it (editing is free) — you
still get **SVG/EPS/PDF + PNG/JPG** and a commercial license. For a premium mark, know you're buying a **recurring,
auto-renewing subscription**, not a one-time logo. Confirm the ladder at design.com — names/prices vary by source.

### "I was charged on renewal / I thought this was a one-time logo purchase"
**Symptom**: An unexpected charge months later, or confusion about what was bought.
**Cause**: Design.com bills a **recurring subscription**, unlike the pay-once-per-logo model of most budget logo
makers; annual plans commit a year upfront and **auto-renew**.
**Solution**: **Cancel from the account dashboard** immediately (self-serve). Refund eligibility depends on **whether
the paid assets were downloaded** and the published window — request fast via design.com/maker/contact with receipts
and screenshots, then dispute with your card issuer if unresolved.

### "My Design.com logo looks like other businesses' logos"
**Symptom**: The mark feels generic or resembles a competitor's.
**Cause**: Concepts come from a **shared template library** and are **non-exclusive by default** — a barely-edited
template looks templated, even though each logo passes a **manual IP review**.
**Solution**: Customize hard — swap the icon, change fonts, palette and layout, using the **AI chat editor** for
plain-language changes. For real uniqueness buy an **exclusive/buyout license** on a supported logo (it removes the
icon from the library), or commission original human work via a contest (`/sales-designcrowd`). Then run
**USPTO/EUIPO** plus a reverse-image check — a commercial license is not a registered trademark.
