# BrandCrowd Platform Help

BrandCrowd (**brandcrowd.com**) is a **designer-supplied logo template marketplace with an editor on top** — real
designers submit finished marks, BrandCrowd curates and **buys** them, and you customize one and license the result.
That supply model, not AI generation, is what makes its rules unlike any peer's.

**Family map.** It began as **Incspring** (2008) → **Brandstack** (2009) → **acquired by DesignCrowd in 2011** and
relaunched as BrandCrowd. Today one Australian entity, **DesignCrowd Pty Ltd**, runs three sites under **one set of
Terms**: **brandcrowd.com** (this — bought designer templates), **design.com** (`/sales-design-com` — AI-first),
**designcrowd.com** (`/sales-designcrowd` — live human contests). Registering on one **may auto-register you on all
three**, which is why users report accounts they never opened.

**The fact behind most bad outcomes: exclusivity here is usually RENTED, not bought.** The Service Terms define **four**
licences — **Free, Standard, Buyout, Exclusive** — each available either **under a subscription** or for a **one-time
fee**. Buy Buyout or Exclusive on a subscription and the rights last only "the duration of your Subscription"; at expiry
the template **goes back on sale** and your licence **automatically downgrades to Standard**. Only the one-time fee is
perpetual, and no exclusivity is retroactive — anyone who licensed that template earlier keeps using it forever. It is
**UI-only: no public API.**

## Step 4 — Actionable guidance

- **Before any exclusivity or ownership answer, establish whether the licence was bought on a SUBSCRIPTION or as a
  one-time fee — and say what happens at expiry.** Under a subscription, Buyout or Exclusive lasts only while the plan is
  paid; at expiry the Service Terms return the template to the library and **automatically downgrade the buyer to a
  Standard licence**. Say plainly that **subscription-bound exclusivity is rented** and **only the one-time fee is
  perpetual** — so **recommend the one-time fee whenever the mark is meant to be permanent**. Add that exclusivity is
  **never retroactive** — prior licensees "retain an ongoing right to license and use" what they created, and some
  shapes/fonts never leave — so **reverse-image-search the template before paying**.
- **Separate the licence from a trademark every time uniqueness comes up.** A **Standard** licence explicitly leaves the
  template on sale "during the term of your Standard License and beyond," so the mark is non-distinctive by
  construction — the exact weakness that sinks a trademark application. Direct the user to run reverse-image plus
  **USPTO/EUIPO** clearance first, and treat a one-time Exclusive licence or original commissioned work as the only
  serious paths to a registrable mark.
- **Quote pricing only as a range, and send the user to checkout for their real number.** **BrandCrowd publishes no
  pricing page** — `/pricing` 404s and no pricing URL appears in its sitemap; price surfaces only inside the checkout
  flow after the design work is done. Third-party sources disagree wildly (roughly **$3–$29/month**) — **explain the
  spread as annual-vs-monthly framing, geo and promotional variants**, never pick one figure as fact. Name tiers by what
  they unlock (entry = hi-res + vector files; mid adds the **website builder**; top adds **link-in-bio + digital business
  card**), and tell the user to check at checkout **both the real price and which licence that price includes**.
- **Say the words "recurring subscription" and "annual commitment" before the user pays.** The usual complaint is a
  purchase that read as one-time turning out to be an annual plan, with **no advance renewal notice and no receipt
  email** — so the charge surfaces on a statement after the window closed. Access isn't guaranteed beyond expiry.
- **For refund problems, work the narrow written policy then the statutory backstop.** Refunds cover a **technical
  impediment** only, expressly refused for "a 'change of mind'" or "design related reasons," at BrandCrowd's discretion.
  So: cancel self-serve from the dashboard → screenshot plan/renewal screens, keep receipts → request via
  brandcrowd.com/maker/contact inside the window → **invoke the Australian Consumer Law guarantees**, then card-dispute.
- **Tell pre-launch founders not to type a confidential name into BrandCrowd before filing.** The Service Terms let
  BrandCrowd use "aspects of Your Content" — **prompts, business names, search terms, written content, photos and edited
  designs** — to **train their AI processes**, including with third-party providers. If the name must stay secret until
  launch, keep it out of the generators and search box — and warn that **one signup may register the same account across
  designcrowd.com.au, design.com and brandcrowd.com**, so the data isn't confined to the site they typed it into.
- **On any ownership, file or "can I use this" question, flag the embedded third-party stock.** Templates may contain
  **Getty/Unsplash** content that **may not be used standalone** and whose "right to use… **may be revoked at any
  time**" with the content removed from your work — so prefer marks built from native template artwork.
- **Rule out the reseller model explicitly when an agency asks.** Under every licence a New Work "cannot be sold more
  than once" or "on any other design platforms" — a resale product or Fiverr/Etsy listings are out, though
  Buyout/Exclusive allow **sublicensing** to a client, the legitimate agency path.
- **There is NO public API — don't plan a pipeline around it.** BrandCrowd is operated through its interface only: no
  REST endpoints, webhooks, Zapier/Make, MCP, or self-serve white-label (the affiliate programme is referral revenue,
  and `/api/`, `/maker/api` are internal front-end AJAX), so generation **cannot be scripted**. Invent no endpoints; for
  volume work use an **image-generation service with a documented API** and vectorize downstream.
- **For designers selling in: get the number in writing first.** BrandCrowd **buys** logos outright (PayPal/Payoneer) with **no published rate card**, won't buy **wordmarks or text-only logos**, curates selectively, reviews over "a few days to a few weeks", and bars resale elsewhere afterwards.

## Gotchas

*Best-effort from research (2026-07) — prices, plan names and refund terms move, and sources disagree badly on the tier
ladder. Licence clauses are quoted from the Service Terms as published 2026-07; confirm at brandcrowd.com.*

- **Subscription-bought exclusivity is RENTED.** A Buyout or Exclusive licence bought under a subscription lasts only
  "the duration of your Subscription"; at expiry the template returns to the library and **your licence automatically
  changes to a Standard License**. Only a **one-time licence fee** is perpetual — and nothing in checkout says so.
- **Exclusivity isn't retroactive, and some elements never leave.** Prior licensees "retain an ongoing right" to their
  versions forever, certain shapes/fonts are never removed, and BrandCrowd and the designer may keep showing the template
  in marketing and portfolios — so reverse-image-search first.
- **Standard licence = the template stays on sale to everyone**, "during the term of your Standard License and beyond." A licence is not a trademark and a shared template is non-distinctive — clear it (reverse-image + USPTO/EUIPO) first.
- **No pricing page exists.** `/pricing` 404s; no pricing URL is in the sitemap; price appears only at checkout, after
  you've done the design work. Third-party figures span **~$3–$29/month** for the same ladder — quote a range.
- **Refunds are technical-fault only** — "change of mind" and "design related reasons" are expressly excluded and all
  refunds are discretionary. The real leverage is the **Australian Consumer Law** guarantees the terms preserve (entity:
  DesignCrowd Pty Ltd, Sydney); liability is capped at the greater of $100 or fees paid. Renewals arrive with no advance
  notice or receipt email in many reports — cancel from the dashboard and act fast.
- **Your prompts, business names and search terms may train their AI** — don't type an unfiled brand name in; **embedded Getty/Unsplash elements can't be used standalone and can be revoked at any time.**
- **One signup can register you on designcrowd.com.au, design.com and brandcrowd.com** under a single set of Terms.
- **A New Work can't be sold more than once or listed on any other design platform** — sublicensing to a client is
  allowed on Buyout/Exclusive, a resale business isn't. **No public API, webhooks, Zapier/Make or MCP.**

## Troubleshooting

### "Someone else is using the same logo I paid for"
**Symptom**: A competitor or unrelated business appears with a near-identical mark.
**Cause**: Under a **Standard** licence the template "will remain available to be licensed to other users of The Site
during the term of your Standard License and beyond" — it was never exclusive. Even after buying Exclusive, earlier
licensees keep an ongoing right to their version.
**Solution**: Check which licence you hold and whether it was subscription-bound. Buy a **one-time Exclusive/Buyout
licence** to pull the template for good, accepting it won't claw back earlier users — then reverse-image-search to see
how widely it's deployed. If the footprint is large, commission original work (`/sales-designcrowd`) instead.

### "I can't find BrandCrowd's prices anywhere"
**Symptom**: No pricing page on the site, and review sites quote wildly different numbers.
**Cause**: BrandCrowd genuinely **publishes no pricing page** — `/pricing` returns 404 and no pricing URL appears in its
sitemap. Price is revealed only in the checkout flow, after design work is invested; third-party figures range from about
**$3 to $29/month** across annual-vs-monthly framing, geo and promo variants.
**Solution**: Take any quoted figure as indicative only. Build the design, go to checkout to see the real price and
**which licence it includes**, and screenshot it before paying — that screenshot is your only durable record of the
terms, and confirm monthly vs annual commitment first.

### "My download is watermarked or too low-resolution for print"
**Symptom**: The exported logo is unusable on signage, packaging or apparel.
**Cause**: The free tier delivers **low-resolution and/or watermarked** files; high-resolution and **vector (SVG/EPS/PDF)**
are gated behind a paid plan.
**Solution**: Use **vector for print, signage and apparel**, raster (PNG/JPG) for web and social. Confirm the file set at
checkout, not from reviews — per-tier lists are third-party-sourced. If a template's distinctive element is embedded
**Getty or Unsplash** stock, that right can be revoked at any time.
