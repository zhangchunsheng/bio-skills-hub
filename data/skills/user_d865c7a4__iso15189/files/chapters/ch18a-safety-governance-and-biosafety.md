# Chapter 18A: Safety Governance and Biosafety (安全管理：治理与生物安全)
## Core Idea
Laboratory safety is a managed system, not a set of isolated precautions: risk must be graded (per pathogen/procedure), governed (policy, officer, documentation), engineered into facility design, signaled (hazard labels), reported when it fails (incidents/exposures), instilled through training, and controlled at the point of hazard generation (PPE, housekeeping, aerosol containment). ISO 15190 supplies the detailed safety technical requirements that CNAS-CL02/GB19489 reference; the lab must show objective evidence — not just a policy statement — for every domain below.

## Requirements by Domain

**常用术语及定义 (Terms & Definitions)** — the lab's safety manual must consistently use and define: hazard vs. risk vs. incident vs. accident vs. occupational exposure, so that incident reports and risk assessments are comparable across the organization (p.680-681).

**实验室风险分级 (Risk Grading)** — pathogens/activities are graded into 4 Risk Groups (RG1-RG4) per GB19489, each mapped to a required Biosafety Level (BSL-1~BSL-4) of facility containment. Before any new test, procedure, or agent is introduced, a documented risk assessment is mandatory, reviewed periodically and after any incident (p.682-684).

**管理要求 (Management Requirements)** — CNAS-CL02 §5.10 requires a designated Safety/Biosafety Officer accountable to the lab director, a safety manual covering all hazard classes, hazard identification + risk assessment + control-measure documentation, emergency response plans, and an annual management review of the safety program with committee meeting records (p.685-687).

**安全设计 (Safety Design)** — facility must separate clean/dirty zones, provide dedicated handwash sinks and emergency eyewash/shower near hazard points, use negative-pressure/directional airflow with HEPA exhaust filtration for BSL-2-enhanced/BSL-3 zones, use cleanable/disinfectable surfaces, and back up critical safety equipment (BSCs, freezers holding infectious material) on emergency power (p.688-689).

**员工/程序/文件/检查和记录要求 (Staff/Procedure/Document/Inspection & Record Requirements)** — safety-critical tasks require version-controlled SOPs; scheduled internal safety audits with checklist and closure tracking; records (incidents, training, medical surveillance, equipment certification) retained per document-control policy, with longer retention typical for radiation/occupational-health records (p.690-692).

**危险标识 (Hazard Signage)** — biohazard trefoil, GHS pictograms (corrosive, flammable, oxidizer, toxic, compressed gas), radiation trefoil, laser and electrical-hazard signs must be posted at hazard-area entrances specifying the hazard class, required PPE, and emergency contact; color-coding (red = fire/emergency, yellow = caution, magenta/purple on yellow = radioactive) is standardized (p.693-694).

**事件、伤害、事故和职业性疾病的报告 (Incident/Injury/Accident/Occupational Disease Reporting)** — reporting sequence: (1) immediate first aid/decontamination, (2) immediate verbal notification to supervisor/safety officer, (3) written report within 24 hours, (4) root-cause investigation, (5) corrective action, (6) medical surveillance follow-up (e.g., needlestick serology at baseline/6 weeks/12 weeks/6 months), (7) external notification to occupational-health/CDC authority when legally required. Needlestick protocol: wash, express blood, disinfect (75% ethanol or iodophor), report ≤24 h, assess source, initiate HIV PEP ideally within 2 h (no later than 72 h) (p.695-697).

**培训和个人责任 (Training & Personal Responsibility)** — mandatory pre-independent-work orientation, role-specific hazard training, annual refresher, and training whenever new equipment/reagent/procedure is introduced, all with signed competency records; staff personally responsible for hazard reporting, correct PPE use, refusing unsafe work, and participating in health surveillance (p.698-699).

**服装和个人防护装备 (Clothing & PPE)** — PPE is task-matched: routine bench work = gloves + lab coat; aerosol-generating tasks = gloves + gown + face shield/goggles + respirator (e.g., N95); chemical handling = chemical-resistant gloves + goggles + apron. Lab coats stay fastened and inside the lab (never worn to cafeteria); closed-toe shoes, tied-back hair, no dangling jewelry; correct donning/doffing sequence prevents self-contamination; reusable PPE (goggles) disinfected between users (p.700-702).

**良好内务和安全工作行为 (Good Housekeeping & Safe Work Practices)** — no eating/drinking/smoking/cosmetics/contact-lens handling in work areas, no mouth pipetting, no food storage in lab refrigerators; benches decontaminated before/after work and after spills; walkways clear; all containers labeled; hand hygiene before/after gloving and before leaving the lab (p.703-704).

**气溶胶与生物安全设施设备 (Aerosols & Biosafety Facilities/Equipment)** — aerosol-generating procedures (vortexing, sonication, uncapping blood cultures, unsealed-rotor centrifugation) require primary containment (BSC) or added PPE. Biosafety Cabinets are classed I/II(A1,A2,B1,B2)/III per protection scope (personnel-only / personnel+environment+product / maximum containment glovebox for BSL-4). Certification is required at installation, after relocation/HEPA change/major repair, and at minimum annually thereafter (HEPA integrity/DOP test, airflow velocity, smoke-pattern test). Centrifuges for infectious material need sealed rotors/safety cups, closed lids during spin, and full stop before opening. Autoclave validation: physical parameters (T/P/time) every cycle, chemical indicator every load, biological indicator (Geobacillus stearothermophilus spore strip) at least monthly and for loads of uncertain sterilization difficulty; standard cycle ≥121°C/15-20 min or 132-134°C for wrapped loads (p.705-709).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Risk Group → Biosafety Level mapping**: GB19489 4-tier RG classification (RG1-RG4) is matched 1:1 to facility containment tier (BSL-1~BSL-4). When to use: at the point of introducing any new organism/specimen type/procedure. How: classify pathogen risk (individual + community), select minimum facility/equipment/practice tier, document in risk assessment (p.682-684).
- **BSC Class selection framework**: Class I (personnel/environment protection only, no product protection) vs Class II A1/A2/B1/B2 (personnel+environment+product, HEPA recirculation ratios differ) vs Class III (glovebox, absolute containment). When to use: selecting containment equipment for a given RG/BSL. How: match class to required protection scope and chemical-vapor exhaust needs (B-type cabinets for volatile chemical work) (p.706-707).
- **Incident reporting cascade**: immediate care → verbal notice → written report (≤24 h) → investigation → corrective action → medical follow-up → external notification. When to use: any occupational exposure/incident/accident. How: sequential, time-boxed steps documented in the incident record (p.695-697).

## Key Concepts
- **Hazard (危险/危险源)** — potential source of harm [p.680].
- **Risk (风险)** — probability × severity of harm from a hazard [p.680].
- **Risk Group, RG (危险度等级)** — WHO/GB19489 pathogen classification RG1-RG4 [p.682-683].
- **Biosafety Level, BSL (生物安全防护水平)** — facility containment tier matched to RG [p.683-684].
- **Aerosol (气溶胶)** — airborne fine particle/droplet suspension; primary lab-acquired-infection route [p.705].
- **Biosafety Cabinet, BSC (生物安全柜)** — HEPA-filtered ventilated enclosure, Class I/II/III [p.706-707].
- **Occupational exposure (职业暴露)** — work-related contact with infectious/hazardous agent [p.695-696].
- **PPE (个人防护装备)** — task-matched protective equipment [p.700].
- **Biological indicator (生物指示剂)** — spore-strip sterilization validator [p.708-709].

## Mental Models
- Think of RG→BSL mapping as a lock-and-key system: the pathogen's risk profile (the lock) determines the minimum facility/equipment tier (the key) — never operate above the pathogen's RG in a lower BSL.
- Use the incident-reporting cascade as a stopwatch: every step (verbal notice, written report, PEP initiation) has a clock running from the moment of exposure; delay at any step compounds risk and audit findings.
- Treat the BSC as a "product+environment+operator" triangle — Class I only protects two vertices (operator+environment); Class II protects all three; select accordingly rather than defaulting to whatever cabinet is available.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating risk assessment as a one-time paperwork exercise**: assessors expect assessment updated when tests/agents/procedures change and after every incident — a static, undated risk assessment is a common nonconformity.
- **Using a Class I BSC for procedures needing product protection**: fails to protect the sample/culture from environmental contamination, invalidating results even if staff are protected.
- **Skipping the ≤24-hour written incident report** because verbal notice was given: verbal-only reporting breaks the audit trail and delays medical follow-up/PEP timing.
- **Annual-only BSC certification without post-relocation/filter-change recheck**: certification date being "within the year" is not sufficient if the cabinet was moved or serviced since the last certificate.

## Reference Tables

**Risk Group → BSL mapping**
| Risk Group | Individual/Community Risk | Example | Required BSL |
|---|---|---|---|
| RG1 | Low/none | Non-pathogenic organisms | BSL-1 |
| RG2 | Moderate individual, limited community | Common bacteria, HBV | BSL-2 (most clinical labs) |
| RG3 | High individual, low community | M. tuberculosis, HIV culture | BSL-3 |
| RG4 | High individual & community | Ebola-type agents | BSL-4 (referral only) |

**Biosafety Cabinet classes**
| Class | Protects | Airflow/HEPA | Certification frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Personnel + environment (not product) | Non-recirculated exhaust HEPA | Installation, post-relocation/filter change, ≥1×/year |
| II (A1/A2/B1/B2) | Personnel + environment + product | Recirculated + exhaust HEPA (B-type ducted for chemical vapors) | Same as above |
| III | Maximum containment (glovebox, BSL-4) | Fully sealed, double HEPA | Same as above |

**Needlestick/exposure follow-up timeline**
| Step | Timing |
|---|---|
| First aid (wash, express blood, disinfect 75% ethanol/iodophor) | Immediate |
| Verbal report to supervisor | Immediate |
| Written incident report | ≤24 hours |
| HIV PEP initiation (if indicated) | Ideally ≤2 h, no later than 72 h |
| Serology follow-up | Baseline, 6 weeks, 12 weeks, 6 months |

|---|---|

## Worked Example
An assessor reviews a hematology lab's incident log and finds a needlestick reported verbally to the supervisor but with no written report on file, and no serology follow-up documented. Root cause: staff believed verbal notice sufficient. Finding written up as nonconformity against the reporting-cascade requirement (p.695-697) — corrective action: revise SOP to mandate the ≤24 h written report and link it to a medical-surveillance tracking checklist triggered automatically upon any sharps-injury entry.

## Key Takeaways
- Safety governance requires a named accountable officer, a living (not static) risk assessment, and closed-loop internal audits — not just a manual on a shelf.
- RG/BSL classification governs facility and equipment choice; BSC class must match required protection scope, not just availability.
- Incident/exposure reporting is time-boxed at every stage; missing the ≤24 h written report or PEP window is a frequent, high-severity nonconformity.
