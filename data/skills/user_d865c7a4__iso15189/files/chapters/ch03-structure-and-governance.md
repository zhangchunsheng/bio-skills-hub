# Chapter 3: Structural and Governance Requirements (结构和管理要求)

## Core Idea
Clause 5 (结构和管理要求) is the organizational "skeleton" of the standard: it establishes legal accountability, named leadership, defined scope of activities, documented authority, measurable objectives, and — new in the 2022 edition — an explicit, continuous risk-management discipline; the authors note assessors typically check clause 5 first via document review before touching any technical clause, because everything downstream depends on this governance layer being real rather than nominal.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Legal-entity-or-defined-part test (clause 5.1)**: the medical laboratory must either be a legal entity itself, or a clearly defined, documented part of one (e.g., a hospital department), with internal authorization documents specifying its delegated legal responsibility. Use when the lab is subordinate to a larger parent organization.
- **Director accountability-with-delegation model (clause 5.2)**: one or more named, competent individual(s) hold overall responsibility/authority for the lab; specific duties may be delegated to named, equally competent designees, but ultimate accountability is not fully transferable. Use whenever a director is unavailable or duties are distributed.
- **"Wherever performed" scope rule (clause 5.3)**: activities under the accredited scope must meet ISO 15189 requirements regardless of location — main site, off-site, temporary/mobile facility, or staff working at a patient's home. Use to build a complete inventory of every location where accredited activities occur.
- **Named-authority principle (clause 5.4)**: authority to develop/authorize the management system, initiate corrective action, or halt testing when risk is unacceptable must be assigned to specific named individuals/roles, not departments in the abstract. Use when documenting who can stop work or approve system changes.
- **SMART quality objectives cascade (clause 5.5)**: top management sets measurable, time-bound quality objectives and policies, communicates and ensures implementation at all levels, and reviews them against real performance data. Use when drafting or reviewing annual quality objectives.
- **Probability × severity risk framework (clause 5.6, new in 2022)**: a documented process identifies risks that could adversely affect patients across pre-examination, examination, and post-examination activities, and drives mitigation actions, applied continuously rather than as an annual exercise.

## Key Concepts
- **legal entity (法律实体)** — the lab, or the legal entity of which it is a defined part, must be legally responsible for its own activities [clause 5.1].
- **laboratory director (实验室主任)** — individual(s) with defined competence and delegated responsibility/authority for lab services, covering professional, scientific, consultative, organizational, and administrative oversight [clause 5.2].
- **laboratory activities (实验室活动)** — activities performed under the accredited scope, at any location, that must conform to the standard and meet user needs [clause 5.3].
- **structure and authority (结构和权限)** — the organized management arrangement that safeguards impartiality and defines interrelationships and named authorities for management-system functions [clause 5.4].
- **objectives and policies (目标和方针)** — top management's documented, measurable quality objectives and policies, communicated and periodically reviewed against performance [clause 5.5].
- **risk management (风险管理)** — a documented, continuous process to identify and mitigate patient-affecting risks across the whole testing pathway [clause 5.6].
- **delegation of authority** — a written record naming a designee, the scope, and the duration of delegated director duties, without transferring ultimate accountability.

## Mental Models
- Think of clause 5.1 as "**paper trail of legal standing**" — a hospital-embedded lab's practice license alone is not enough; there must be an internal document tracing the lab's delegated authority from the parent legal entity.
- Use the "**who can stop the line?**" test for clause 5.4 — if the answer to "who has authority to halt testing when risk is unacceptable" is a department name rather than a person/role, the control is incomplete.
- Treat clause 5.5 objectives as **falsifiable claims**: an objective must be phrased so that "did we meet it?" has a yes/no answer backed by data, not a sentiment.
- Think of clause 5.6 risk management as a **pathway-wide lens**, not a phase-specific one — risks must be tracked across pre-examination, examination, and post-examination stages as a single continuous view, mirroring the harm/hazard/risk vocabulary from Chapter 1.

## Anti-patterns
- **Relying solely on a parent hospital's practice license without an internal delegation document**: leaves the lab's own legal accountability under clause 5.1 undocumented for its specific accredited scope.
- **Naming only a department, not a person, as holding stop-work or corrective-action authority**: fails the clause 5.4 expectation that assessors can get a specific named answer.
- **Setting vague quality objectives ("continuously improve quality") with no measurable target or review cadence**: cannot be verified as met or not met at management review, undermining clause 5.5.
- **Treating clause 5.6 risk management as an annual paperwork exercise** disconnected from daily operations: contradicts its intent as a new, continuous, pathway-wide discipline in the 2022 edition.
- **Failing to inventory off-site/mobile/home-visit activities** under the same management-system controls as the main site: a gap against the "wherever performed" scope rule in clause 5.3.

## Worked Example
A laboratory operates as a department within a hospital and holds no independent practice license of its own. Under clause 5.1, this is acceptable only if the lab can produce an internal document — issued by the parent legal entity — that explicitly defines the lab as a recognized part of that entity with delegated responsibility for its accredited activities. Separately, for clause 5.6, the authors illustrate risk management with two paired examples along the testing pathway: a **pre-examination** risk (specimen mislabeling) mitigated by a barcode-based positive patient identification system, and a **post-examination** risk (delayed critical-value notification) mitigated by an automated LIS alert combined with an escalation call tree — showing risk management applied as a continuous mechanism rather than a single document.

## Key Takeaways
1. A subordinate lab needs its own internal delegation document from the parent legal entity — a shared practice license is not sufficient evidence for clause 5.1.
2. Director accountability cannot be fully delegated away; delegation records must specify scope, duration, and the designee's matching competence.
3. Every location where accredited activities occur — including mobile units and home visits — must be inventoried and governed by the same management-system controls.
4. Authorities to halt testing, approve corrective action, or change the management system must be assigned to named individuals, not departments.
5. Quality objectives must be measurable and time-bound, reviewed against actual performance data at management review — not aspirational statements.
6. Risk management (5.6) is a new, continuous, pathway-wide requirement in ISO 15189:2022, not a periodic compliance exercise, and reuses the harm/hazard/risk definitions from clause 3.
