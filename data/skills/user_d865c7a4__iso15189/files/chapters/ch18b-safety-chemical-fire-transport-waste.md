# Chapter 18B: Chemical, Fire, Electrical Safety, Specimen Transport & Waste Disposal (安全管理：化学、消防、电气、运送与废物处置)
## Core Idea
The second half of laboratory safety governance turns from biological/procedural risk to physical-hazard control (chemical, radiation, fire, electrical) and to the two points where hazards leave the controlled environment — specimen transport and waste disposal — where packaging, labeling, and time limits are strictly numeric and are the most commonly cited assessment nonconformities.

## Requirements by Domain

**化学品安全 (Chemical Safety)** — a current chemical inventory must exist, with an SDS/MSDS accessible in the local language for every hazardous chemical, and staff trained to read it; inventory and SDS updated whenever a new chemical enters use. Storage must segregate incompatible classes: acids away from bases and from oxidizers/flammables; flammables in an approved flammable-storage cabinet away from ignition sources; corrosives stored below eye level; compressed-gas cylinders secured upright with restraint straps/chains, valve caps on when not in use. Chemical spill kits must be available, and fume hoods used for volatile/toxic work with periodic face-velocity verification (≈0.4-0.6 m/s) (p.710-712).

**放射安全 (Radiation Safety)** — controlled areas (受控区) must be demarcated and posted with the radiation trefoil; staff issued personal dosimeters read periodically (e.g., monthly/quarterly); the ALARA ("As Low As Reasonably Achievable") principle governs dose minimization; sealed sources leak-tested periodically (e.g., every 6 months); radioactive waste segregated and decay-stored per isotope half-life before clearance; annual radiation-safety training required (p.713).

**消防安全与消防设备设施 (Fire Safety & Equipment)** — a fire risk assessment identifies fire classes present per room; extinguishers are type-matched to those classes (dry powder/CO2/foam for A/B/C-class fires), placed at accessible points, visually inspected monthly (每月) and professionally serviced/pressure-tested annually (每年). Fire exits must be clearly marked, unobstructed, and illuminated with emergency lighting; evacuation-route diagrams posted; fire drills conducted at least annually; alarms/smoke detectors tested periodically; flammable/combustible chemical storage volumes capped per room, and sprinkler heads never obstructed (p.715-718).

**电气设备与安全 (Electrical Equipment & Safety)** — equipment undergoes periodic inspection/testing (visual + earth-leakage/portable-appliance-type test), typically annually; damaged cords/plugs are removed from service immediately; multi-plug daisy-chaining and overloaded circuits are prohibited; equipment must be grounded, operation with wet hands prohibited, and equipment near water sources protected (e.g., GFCI/residual-current protection) (p.719-720).

**样本的运送 (Specimen Transport)** — routine diagnostic specimens ship as UN3373 "Biological Substance, Category B" using triple packaging: (1) primary watertight/leak-proof receptacle, (2) absorbent material between primary and secondary sufficient to absorb the entire contents in case of leakage, (3) secondary watertight/leak-proof packaging, all inside (4) rigid outer packaging marked with the UN3373 diamond and "Biological Substance, Category B" text; package volume is regulated (≤500 mL/g per package for surface transport). Specimens/isolates of a pathogen capable of causing permanent disability or fatal disease in otherwise healthy humans/animals on exposure are reclassified as Category A (UN2814 human / UN2900 animal), requiring the more stringent P620 packing instruction with drop-test-certified packaging — used for confirmed/suspected highly pathogenic isolates, not routine specimens. Internal (intra-facility) transport requires a leak-proof secondary container labeled biohazard, carried by designated staff or via a biohazard-validated pneumatic-tube system; temperature/time-sensitive analytes require temperature-controlled transport with chain-of-custody documentation (p.721-723).

**废物处置 (Waste Disposal)** — medical waste (医疗废物) is segregated at the point of origin into 5 national categories: 感染性废物 (infectious), 病理性废物 (pathological), 损伤性废物 (sharps), 药物性废物 (pharmaceutical), 化学性废物 (chemical). All (except sharps) go into yellow bags/containers (黄色包装物) bearing the biohazard symbol; sharps go into rigid, puncture-resistant sharps containers (利器盒). Bags are filled to ≤3/4 capacity, sealed with a gooseneck tie, and labeled with source department, date, and category. On-site temporary storage must be in a dedicated, secured, cool holding area, with removal for centralized disposal within no more than 2 days (不超过2天) per the national Medical Waste Management Regulation (医疗废物管理条例), unless refrigerated storage extends the locally approved limit. Waste hand-over to the licensed disposal contractor requires a signed transfer manifest (交接登记), retained for at least 3 years. Liquid infectious waste (blood/culture fluid) is chemically disinfected (chlorine-based, ≥2000 mg/L, ≥30 min contact) before drain disposal, or else collected as infectious waste. Chemical and radioactive waste are disposed via licensed contractors, with radioactive waste decay-stored per isotope half-life before clearance (p.724-726).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Chemical incompatibility segregation matrix**: acids ⟷ bases ⟷ oxidizers/flammables kept physically separated; corrosives stored low, compressed gases restrained upright. When to use: designing/auditing any chemical storage area. How: cross-reference SDS incompatibility data per chemical pair before co-locating (p.711-712).
- **Triple-packaging system (UN3373/P620)**: primary receptacle + absorbent + secondary packaging + rigid outer packaging, scaled up to P620 drop-test certification for Category A. When to use: any specimen leaving the facility. How: classify specimen risk (Category A vs B) first, then select the matching packaging instruction and volume limit (p.721-723).
- **Medical-waste 5-category segregation**: 感染性/病理性/损伤性/药物性/化学性, each with defined container type (yellow bag vs rigid sharps box) and a ≤2-day on-site storage clock. When to use: at every point of waste generation. How: segregate at source, label, store ≤2 days, hand over with signed manifest (p.724-726).

## Key Concepts
- **SDS/MSDS (安全数据表/化学品安全技术说明书)** — hazard/handling/first-aid/disposal document required for every hazardous chemical [p.710].
- **ALARA** — dose-minimization principle for radiation safety [p.713].
- **Fire class (火灾种类, A/B/C)** — combustible-material category determining extinguisher type [p.716].
- **UN3373 (生物物质，B类)** — packaging code for routine Category B diagnostic specimens [p.721-722].
- **Category A infectious substance (A类感染性物质, UN2814/UN2900)** — pathogen capable of causing permanent disability/fatal disease on exposure; P620 packaging [p.722].
- **Medical waste (医疗废物)** — 5-category regulated waste stream (感染性/病理性/损伤性/药物性/化学性) [p.724].
- **Transfer manifest (交接登记)** — signed record of waste hand-over to disposal contractor, retained ≥3 years [p.725-726].
- **Decay storage (衰变储存)** — holding radioactive waste until activity drops below clearance level per half-life [p.713, 726].

## Mental Models
- Think of specimen packaging as concentric defense rings: even if the primary receptacle fails, absorbent + secondary + rigid outer must still contain the leak — auditors test this logic, not just presence of three layers.
- Use the "≤2-day clock" for medical waste like a perishable-goods shelf life: storage location, temperature, and hand-over paperwork all exist to prove that clock was respected.
- Treat fire-extinguisher inspection as two nested cycles: a fast monthly visual check (pressure gauge, seal, accessibility) nested inside a slower annual professional service — missing the fast cycle is the more common finding.

## Anti-patterns
- **Storing routine specimens in Category A (P620) packaging "to be safe"**: misclassification suggests the lab doesn't understand risk-based packaging selection, prompting the assessor to probe deeper into training records.
- **Mixing sharps into yellow infectious-waste bags instead of rigid sharps containers**: puncture risk to waste handlers is a direct nonconformity against the segregation-by-container-type rule.
- **Holding medical waste on-site beyond 2 days without refrigeration or documented local exception**: the storage-time limit is a hard numeric criterion, not a guideline — exceeding it without justification is cited even if disposal eventually occurs correctly.
- **Relying on visual "looks fine" checks for fire extinguishers without a documented monthly log**: absence of a dated inspection record is treated as absence of the inspection itself.
- **Co-storing flammable solvents with oxidizing acids in the same unsegregated cabinet**: incompatibility segregation failure is a common storage-area nonconformity even when SDS sheets are otherwise present.

## Worked Example
During a mock assessment, the reviewer finds a sharps container in the phlebotomy area filled past 3/4 capacity and a yellow infectious-waste bag holding several uncapped needles. Two nonconformities are raised: (1) sharps improperly segregated into a non-puncture-resistant container (violates 损伤性废物 segregation rule), and (2) waste-fill-level rule (≤3/4) exceeded, increasing spill/puncture risk during transport to the ≤2-day holding area. Corrective action: retrain phlebotomy staff on the 5-category segregation table and add a fill-line visual indicator to sharps containers.

## Key Takeaways
- Chemical storage safety hinges on incompatibility segregation (acids/bases/oxidizers/flammables) verified against SDS data, not just SDS availability.
- Specimen transport classification (Category A vs B) is risk-driven and packaging-specific; misclassification in either direction is a nonconformity.
- Medical waste rules are numeric and auditable: 5-category segregation, ≤3/4 fill, ≤2-day on-site storage, ≥3-year manifest retention — these are the figures assessors check first.
