# Chapter 2: General Requirements (总体要求)

## Core Idea
Clause 4 (总体要求) establishes three foundational obligations — impartiality, confidentiality, and patient-related requirements — that sit above and govern every technical clause that follows; the authors frame clause 4 as the standard's "constitutional layer," meaning assessors expect these to be demonstrably embedded into governance and daily practice, not merely stated in a standalone policy document.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Impartiality risk cycle (clause 4.1)**: the lab must (1) continually identify risks to impartiality arising from its activities, relationships, or personnel, and (2) demonstrate elimination or minimization of any risk found — an ongoing cycle, not a one-time policy declaration. Use whenever evaluating new business relationships, referral arrangements, or staff personal/financial ties.
- **Confidentiality-by-legally-enforceable-commitment (clause 4.2)**: the lab must manage confidentiality of all information obtained/created through lab activities via legally enforceable commitments extending to committees, contractors, external bodies, and any individual acting on the lab's behalf — not just direct employees. Use when onboarding any external party (IT vendor, student, cleaning staff) who may access lab information.
- **Patient-need-accessibility test (clause 4.3)**: service accessibility and design must be evaluated against actual patient needs, including urgency of care, advisory support on test selection/interpretation, and a patient-specific complaint channel. Use when designing or reviewing service scope, hours, and communication channels.

## Key Concepts
- **impartiality (公正性)** — the lab shall carry out activities impartially and organize/manage itself to safeguard this, continually identifying and mitigating risks from activities, relationships, or personnel [clause 4.1].
- **confidentiality (保密性)** — a legally enforceable commitment to manage confidentiality, at all levels, of information obtained or created through lab activities, covering both electronic and paper records [clause 4.2].
- **conflict of interest (COI) register** — the documented log of identified impartiality risks (financial, relationship-based, or commercial-pressure) and their mitigations; not named as a defined term in the standard itself but the practical artifact the authors say assessors expect.
- **patient-related requirements (患者相关的要求)** — the lab shall ensure accessible services meeting patient needs, including urgency of care, advisory input, and complaint handling [clause 4.3].
- **legally required information release** — a carve-out within confidentiality allowing disclosure mandated by law (e.g., statutory infectious disease reporting), which must be pre-defined in policy rather than decided ad hoc.
- **information access restriction** — internal access to patient/laboratory information must be limited to those who need it for their specific activity, applied to both LIS/electronic and paper records.

## Mental Models
- Think of clause 4.1 as "**disclosure is not mitigation**" — merely writing an impartiality policy is insufficient; the lab must show the identified risk and the specific action taken (e.g., staff recusal from vendor selection).
- Use the **"who else besides employees?"** check for clause 4.2 — confidentiality commitments must reach contractors, students, and any third party with information access, since the standard explicitly extends beyond staff.
- Treat clause 4.3 as **"accessibility ≠ availability"** — a service can technically exist but still fail patient needs if urgency handling, interpretive advice, or a complaint mechanism is missing.

## Anti-patterns
- **Having an impartiality policy but no COI register or periodic re-review**: assessors interview staff to probe awareness; a policy with no evidence of ongoing risk identification reads as a paperwork exercise.
- **Limiting confidentiality agreements to full-time employees**: contractors, IT vendors, and students with system/data access left uncovered is a documented gap against clause 4.2's explicit "all levels" wording.
- **No defined patient complaint channel distinct from the general complaint-handling clause**: clause 4.3 expects patient-specific accessibility, so folding it entirely into a generic later-clause complaint process without patient-facing visibility is a weak response.
- **Treating clause 4 as a one-page front-matter policy** rather than an operating discipline reflected in COI registers, access logs, and complaint records reviewed at management review.

## Reference Tables
| Sub-clause | Core obligation | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 公正性 (impartiality) | Identify and mitigate risks to impartiality on an ongoing basis | COI register, top-management impartiality commitment, periodic review records, staff interview readiness |
| 4.2 保密性 (confidentiality) | Legally enforceable confidentiality at all levels, incl. third parties | Signed confidentiality agreements (staff + contractors/students), access control logs, disclosure policy incl. legal-mandate carve-outs |
| 4.3 患者相关的要求 (patient-related requirements) | Accessible services meeting patient needs incl. urgency and advisory input | Patient communication channels, interpretation/advisory availability, patient complaint procedure, satisfaction data |

## Worked Example
A staff member's spouse works as a sales representative for a reagent supplier used by the lab. The authors flag this as a textbook clause 4.1 impartiality risk: financial/relationship-based, arising from a personnel connection rather than the lab's own commercial activity. The expected response is not to ignore or merely note the relationship, but to record it in a COI register and apply a specific mitigation — for example, recusing that staff member from vendor-selection decisions — with the mitigation itself documented and revisited at subsequent reviews, closing the loop from "risk identified" to "risk minimized."

## Key Takeaways
1. Clause 4 requirements must be visible in operational artifacts (COI registers, access logs, complaint records), not just a front-matter policy statement.
2. Impartiality risk identification is continuous; a static policy without periodic re-review will not satisfy 4.1.
3. Confidentiality commitments must extend to every external party with information access, not only direct employees.
4. Any statutory/legally mandated information disclosure must be pre-defined in policy, not improvised case-by-case.
5. Patient-related requirements (4.3) call for a patient-specific complaint channel and advisory capability, not just general service availability.
6. Expect assessors to test clause 4 via staff interviews as much as document review — awareness matters as much as paperwork.
