# Dosha Framework

Use this reference to collect answers, score likely Ayurvedic constitution patterns, and choose gentle suggestions.

## Quality Limits

This is an informal reflective screen, not a validated Prakriti assessment scale. Never present the result as official, diagnostic, or equivalent to assessment by a trained Ayurvedic clinician.

For a higher-quality conversation:

- Ask whether the answers describe the user's long-term baseline rather than only the last few weeks.
- Track possible confounders such as acute illness, pregnancy, medication changes, endocrine conditions, major weight change, shift work, travel, grief, or sleep disruption.
- Separate `prakriti` language for stable constitution from `vikriti` language for current imbalance.
- Cap confidence at "medium" for ordinary self-report. Use "high for this informal screen" only when many stable answers strongly converge and no major confounder is present.

## Intake Questionnaire

Ask 10-15 of these questions. For each, map mostly A to Vata, B to Pitta, and C to Kapha. Let the user choose mixed answers.

1. Body frame:
   - A: light, narrow, variable weight
   - B: medium, athletic, moderate muscle
   - C: solid, broad, gains weight easily
2. Skin and hair:
   - A: dry, rough, thin, cool
   - B: warm, sensitive, reddish, early graying or thinning
   - C: smooth, oily, thick, cool
3. Temperature preference:
   - A: dislikes cold or wind
   - B: dislikes heat or strong sun
   - C: dislikes damp, heaviness, or cold humidity
4. Appetite:
   - A: variable or forgets meals
   - B: strong, sharp, irritable when hungry
   - C: steady but can skip meals
5. Digestion:
   - A: gas, bloating, irregularity
   - B: acidity, loose stools, heat
   - C: heaviness, slow digestion
6. Energy pattern:
   - A: bursts of energy, tires quickly
   - B: intense, focused, can overdo
   - C: steady, enduring, slow to start
7. Sleep:
   - A: light, interrupted, difficulty falling asleep
   - B: moderate, wakes hot or alert
   - C: deep, long, hard to wake
8. Mind under stress:
   - A: anxious, scattered, worried
   - B: impatient, critical, driven
   - C: withdrawn, stuck, avoidant
9. Speech and pace:
   - A: fast, changeable, animated
   - B: precise, direct, persuasive
   - C: calm, slow, measured
10. Learning and memory:
   - A: learns quickly, forgets quickly
   - B: sharp comprehension, strong focus
   - C: learns slowly, remembers well
11. Movement preference:
   - A: likes variety, dance, walking, light movement
   - B: likes challenge, sports, goals
   - C: likes strength, endurance, steady routines
12. Routine:
   - A: irregular schedule, benefits from structure
   - B: structured, goal-oriented, can become intense
   - C: stable routine, can become sedentary
13. Seasonal pattern:
   - A: worse in cold, dry, windy weather
   - B: worse in hot, humid, intense weather
   - C: worse in cold, damp, heavy weather
14. Emotional strengths:
   - A: creative, adaptable, enthusiastic
   - B: courageous, organized, discerning
   - C: patient, loyal, grounded
15. Common imbalance signs:
   - A: restlessness, dry skin, constipation, worry
   - B: irritability, inflammation, reflux, heat
   - C: sluggishness, congestion, heaviness, low motivation

## Scoring

Count each clear A/B/C as one point for Vata/Pitta/Kapha. Split mixed answers across doshas when needed. If an answer reflects a recent temporary state, mark it as possible vikriti rather than stable constitution.

Confidence guide:

- High for informal screen: 12 or more answered, one or two doshas clearly lead, answers are consistent over many years, and no major confounder is present.
- Medium: 8-11 answered, some mixed answers, or a clear leading pattern with minor uncertainty.
- Low: fewer than 8 answered, many "not sure" answers, major current-life confounders, or current symptoms dominate baseline traits.

Do not calculate percentages unless the user asks. If percentages are useful, present them as "share of answers in this informal screen," not as body composition or medical measurement.

## Nutrition Suggestions

Keep suggestions ordinary, flexible, and food-based.

Vata-leading patterns:

- Favor warm, cooked, moist, grounding meals at regular times.
- Consider soups, stews, porridges, root vegetables, rice, oats, lentils, ghee or olive oil if tolerated, and warm drinks.
- Reduce excess cold, dry, raw, irregular, or highly stimulating patterns when they worsen symptoms.

Pitta-leading patterns:

- Favor cooling, steady, satisfying meals and avoid skipping meals.
- Consider leafy greens, cucumber, melons, grains, legumes, cilantro, mint, coconut, and moderate healthy fats if tolerated.
- Reduce excess chili, fried foods, alcohol, very sour foods, and heat exposure when they aggravate symptoms.

Kapha-leading patterns:

- Favor lighter, warm, well-spiced meals and avoid grazing when not hungry.
- Consider legumes, cooked greens, barley, millet, apples, pears, ginger, black pepper, and bitter or astringent vegetables if tolerated.
- Reduce excess heavy, oily, sugary, cold, or very dairy-rich patterns when they increase heaviness.

Dual patterns:

- Combine the two leading doshas and prioritize the dosha that appears currently aggravated.
- When advice conflicts, choose the gentler middle path: regular meals, simple cooked foods, adequate protein, hydration, and seasonal adjustment.

Unclear or tridoshic patterns:

- Avoid forcing a type.
- Offer a balanced plan: regular meals, adequate sleep, moderate movement, hydration, simple cooked foods, and attention to seasonal comfort.
- Suggest observing which conditions reliably aggravate the user: cold/dry/irregularity, heat/intensity, or damp/heaviness.

## Lifestyle Suggestions

Vata:

- Regular sleep/wake rhythm, warmth, calming evening routine, gentle yoga, walking, breath awareness, fewer late nights.
- Use simple planning and consistent mealtimes to reduce scattered energy.

Pitta:

- Moderate exercise, cooling downtime, breaks from competition, shade in hot weather, relaxed meals, cooling breath practices.
- Build in unscheduled time to soften overfocus and irritability.

Kapha:

- Daily movement, morning light, varied routines, brisk walking or strength work as appropriate, decluttering, energizing music.
- Use small commitments and social accountability to overcome inertia.
