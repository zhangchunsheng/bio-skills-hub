# Evidence Sources

Use this reference to keep the skill grounded in high-quality open or public sources. Prefer official public-health sources for safety and open-access scholarly sources for Prakriti assessment limits.

## Source Hierarchy

1. Official public-health and regulatory sources for safety: WHO, NCCIH/NIH, FDA, poison centers, and equivalent national authorities.
2. Open-access peer-reviewed reviews for Prakriti assessment methods, validation, and limitations.
3. Open data or open-access psychometric projects for questionnaire design ideas, only when license permits reuse and attribution is practical.
4. Official Ayurveda portals and manuals as authority signals, but do not copy restricted questionnaires or present this skill as an official CCRAS/AYUSH tool.
5. Avoid commercial clinic pages, product brands, influencer content, anonymous quizzes, and sources selling supplements or consultations.

## Preferred Open/Public References

Safety and evidence framing:

- NCCIH, "Ayurvedic Medicine: In Depth" - public domain U.S. government overview. Use for: limited evidence language, heavy-metal cautions, and advice not to delay conventional care. https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth
- FDA, "Heavy Metal Poisoning Associated with Certain Unapproved Ayurvedic Drug Products" - U.S. regulatory warning current as of December 2, 2025. Use for: unapproved product cautions, heavy-metal symptoms, Poison Control guidance, and "no FDA-approved ayurvedic products" in the United States. https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fraudulent-products/fda-warns-about-heavy-metal-poisoning-associated-certain-unapproved-ayurvedic-drug-products
- WHO traditional medicine Q&A - public WHO overview. Use for: evidence-based, safety, quality-control, and pharmacovigilance framing. https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/traditional-medicine
- WHO Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine topic page. Use for: people-centered, evidence-based, culturally respectful integration language. https://www.who.int/health-topics/traditional-complementary-and-integrative-medicine
- WHO, "General Guidelines for Methodologies on Research and Evaluation of Traditional Medicine" - public PDF. Use for: the principle that traditional medicine research should consider safety, efficacy, quality, cultural context, and methodology limits. https://www.paho.org/sites/default/files/pm-whotraditional-medicines-research-evaluation_0.pdf

Prakriti assessment quality:

- Venkatesh et al., "Prakriti (constitutional typology) in Ayurveda: a critical review of Prakriti assessment tools and their scientific validity," Frontiers in Medicine, 2025. Open access under CC BY. Use for: caution that Prakriti assessment tools remain partly validated; only a minority of tools had validation; no tool fully met all evaluation criteria in the review. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1656249/full
- Holla and Bhargav, "The Brief Prakriti Inventory: Latent structure, reliability, and validity," Zenodo dataset, 2025. Open under CC BY 4.0. Use for: psychometric design awareness and reproducibility norms, not as a medical instrument. https://zenodo.org/records/17607907

Official Ayurveda resources:

- CCRAS Ayur Prakriti Web Portal page. Use for: recognizing that an official standardized Prakriti assessment effort exists. Do not copy its questionnaire or imply this skill is the portal. https://ccras.nic.in/ayur-prakriti-web-portal/
- CCRAS Manual of Standard Operative Procedures for Prakriti Assessment. Treat as official but not open-source: it says rights are reserved and training is required. Use only to understand why this skill should refer users to trained assessors for official assessment. https://ccras.nic.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/15032023_AYUR-PRAKRITI-WEB-PORTAL-Manual.pdf
- CCRAS Ayurveda Handbooks page. Use cautiously as official Ayurveda context; avoid disease-specific advice and do not turn handbook material into treatment guidance. https://ccras.nic.in/publication/ayurveda-handboooks/

## Evidence Language Rules

- Say "informal reflection" or "wellness-oriented screen," not "diagnosis."
- Say "traditional Ayurvedic framework" when describing Vata, Pitta, Kapha, prakriti, vikriti, dinacharya, or ritucharya.
- Say "evidence is limited/mixed" for disease claims unless a newly checked, high-quality systematic review supports a narrow claim.
- Say "ordinary food and lifestyle suggestions" for recommendations.
- Do not cite a source for a claim it does not support.
- Do not use source authority to recommend products, herbs, supplements, cleanses, fasting, or procedures.

## If Asked for Citations

Give 2-5 relevant links, prioritizing:

- NCCIH and FDA for safety.
- WHO for evidence-based traditional medicine framing.
- Frontiers 2025 review for Prakriti-assessment limitations.
- CCRAS portal only when the user asks about official Prakriti assessment.

Keep citations brief and explain what each source supports.
