# Evidence and safety boundaries

This skill is a consumer sleep-timing aid. It is not a medical device and does not measure blood caffeine concentration.

## Evidence used

- A 5-hour half-life is a population-level working estimate. Reported adult elimination varies substantially, so the skill supports a conservative 7-hour scenario and never calls either value the user's true metabolism. See the 2022 systematic analysis of adult human caffeine pharmacokinetics: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8914174/
- Caffeine can affect sleep even when consumed hours before bedtime. A randomized, double-blind study found disruption from a 400 mg dose taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before bedtime: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3805807/
- The FDA says 400 mg per day is an amount not generally associated with negative effects for most adults, while emphasizing variation in sensitivity and product content: https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much
- ACOG states that moderate caffeine consumption below 200 mg per day during pregnancy does not appear to be a major contributor to miscarriage or preterm birth, but this skill still avoids personalized pregnancy advice: https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy

## Interpretation limits

- The exponential calculation estimates elimination after intake; it does not model absorption, tolerance, genetics, smoking, oral contraceptives, pregnancy-related changes, liver function, or drug interactions.
- The `low`, `medium`, and `high` residue bands are product communication heuristics, not validated clinical cutoffs.
- Drink caffeine values are estimates unless taken from an exact current label or official product specification.
- A lower estimated bedtime residue does not guarantee good sleep; a higher estimate does not guarantee insomnia.

## Mandatory safety response

- Chest pain, fainting, severe palpitations, confusion, seizure-like symptoms, or severe breathing difficulty: stop routine advice and recommend urgent medical help.
- Pregnancy, breastfeeding, minors, medication use, heart rhythm problems, liver disease, epilepsy, severe anxiety, or diagnosed insomnia: avoid a personalized allowance and recommend professional guidance.
- Pure caffeine powder/liquid or energy drinks mixed with alcohol: discourage use; do not optimize dosing.
